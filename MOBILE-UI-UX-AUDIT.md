# Gem Management — mobile UI/UX audit
**16 November 2026 · Checkout `dd93e3f` · Separate from the desktop/site-wide audit**

## Verdict

The refreshed phone shell works: the main bars fit, primary taps are generous, Inventory retains stone IDs and custody, and native sheets keep a visible Close control. The biggest remaining risk is **selection scope**: the app can open a sale with a preselected stone even when the current search shows zero records. Next, fix browser Back with open sheets and make buyer document links distinct. The remaining work is primarily about reachability and clarity, not another visual redesign.

## What passed and should be preserved

- **Reflow:** All **18 Owner workspaces** inspected at **320 × 700** in Chromium dark mode fit their root viewport. The sampled record/table and bar containers did not show unexpected horizontal overflow. The intentionally swipeable Inventory status strip is separate from page overflow.
- **Browser checks:** Nine focused Chromium tests and all five existing WebKit mobile tests passed. In WebKit exploration, filters returned the expected five held rows at **390 × 844**, **430 × 932**, and **844 × 390**. The existing WebKit suite covers setup, sign-in/2FA reveal, safe-area simulation, inventory details, mobile destinations, and mocked AI states.
- **Touch and keyboard:** At 320px, Inventory tab measured **57 × 48 CSS px**, toolbar search **44 × 44**, and Filters **120 × 44** in the sampled state. An 85-step Inventory Tab walk found **no fully covered focused page control** in the fixture. The avatar opener regained focus when its sheet was explicitly closed.
- **Accessibility baseline:** axe found no violations in the sampled 320px dark Owner screens or the 390px light buyer screen. This does not invalidate the separately measured buyer file-link issue or establish conformance. Keep explicit status wording, device-native pickers, and the reduced-motion option. [Apple accessibility guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility).

## Scope and next pass

This audit used local Playwright Chromium and WebKit, touch emulation, and populated synthetic fixture records. Existing mobile browser tests provided additional coverage. No live records were read or changed and no sale, memo, share, or buyer response was submitted by the exploratory probes. Native Safari/Home Screen, actual iOS and Android keyboards, VoiceOver/TalkBack, physical one-handed use, and device text enlargement remain unverified.

**Recommended sequence:** fix M01 and M02 first; then separate buyer file targets and surface the buyer response action; finally split account controls from More and compact the filters/editor states. For device acceptance, test one older or compact phone in portrait, one larger phone in landscape, and a real keyboard-open form before signoff.

## Remediation

The findings above are the pre-fix observations. Inventory now identifies selections outside the current page and requires an ID review before sales, memo, or share actions can use them. The review resets when selection or view context changes. Phone browser Back dismisses the top sheet, keeps the workspace in place, and uses the existing unsaved-changes confirmation when needed. Forward does not restore a dismissed editor.

Buyer reports are separate, full-width touch targets, with a response shortcut above the records. The avatar now opens Account directly; More keeps role-filtered workspace search and recognizes job terms such as “stock,” “supplier,” and “invoice.” Sort has a removable chip, no-match views drop pagination, filters keep Done visible in short phone viewports, and intake groups optional details without dropping entered values. Native keyboards, Home Screen mode, and screen readers still need on-device verification.
